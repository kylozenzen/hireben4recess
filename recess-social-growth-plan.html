Blog + search plan

Build useful local authority that compounds.

The blog should prioritize high-intent parent questions and city-specific planning—not generic parenting commentary that could live on any website.

Six content clusters

  1. High-intent city guides: camps, afterschool and enrichment by age, interest, neighborhood and price.
  2. School-calendar utility: district holidays, teacher workdays, spring/winter break and early release.
  3. Parent decision support: choosing camp, budget planning, siblings, nerves and schedule patchwork.
  4. Expert trust: educators, pediatric voices, child development and inclusive program guidance.
  5. Local discovery: free activities, indoor options, weekend plans and seasonal ideas.
  6. Marketplace stories: providers, school Giveback results, parent wins and Recess data.

A practical publishing rhythm

  • Weekly: one high-intent city or booking article
  • Weekly: one evergreen planning or expert article
  • Biweekly: one provider, school or parent story
  • Monthly: each city’s “what to book now” update
  • Every 6–8 weeks: refresh seasonal guides and availability
  • Quarterly: a data-led State of Family Scheduling story
One strong article becomes a content pack.

1 Reel · 1 TikTok · 1 carousel · 3 Story frames · 2 Pins · 1 Facebook post · 1 email section.

Data-informed decisions

Use the numbers to decide what gets made next.

Reporting should be a working feedback loop—not a monthly autopsy. The purpose is to understand what parents value, what drives action and where the next content opportunity lives.

Step 01

Measure

Capture creative, audience, traffic and registration signals consistently.

Step 02

Understand

Separate empty reach from useful attention, intent and qualified action.

Step 03

Decide

Choose what to repeat, revise, redistribute or stop producing.

Step 04

Create

Turn the learning into stronger briefs, hooks and local content.

Step 05

Test

Change one meaningful variable and give the idea a fair comparison.

Step 06

Improve

Document the learning and carry it into the next planning cycle.

The decision dashboard

One view, four levels of performance.

The dashboard should connect platform behavior to business value instead of treating every metric as equally important.

  • Creative: hook retention, watch time, completion, saves, shares and meaningful comments.
  • Audience: recurring questions, sentiment, response time, follower quality and market-level interest.
  • Intent: profile visits, guide downloads, marketplace search starts and provider-page views.
  • Outcome: registrations, assisted bookings, provider leads, school interest and partner amplification.
Scaling the workflow

More content should not mean more chaos.

As volume grows, a platform such as Buffer can keep the team moving through one shared content calendar and a clear approval process.

  • Centralize drafts, publishing schedules and stakeholder feedback.
  • Use approval workflows for founder, partner or higher-sensitivity content.
  • Use analytics and tools such as an MCP connector to summarize patterns and accelerate research or first drafts—while strategy, voice and final decisions stay human-led.

The centerpiece

My first 30 / 60 / 90 days.

Listen before scaling. Build repeatable systems before chasing volume. Then connect the winning content directly to searches, registrations and partner growth.

Days 1–30

Listen + map

Focus: understand the real operating system
  • Meet founders, growth, product, support, provider and school partnership owners.
  • Audit channels, content, comments, DMs, reviews, search terms and the click-to-book journey.
  • Build a parent-question taxonomy from support conversations, reviews and marketplace searches.
  • Map school calendars, seasonal booking windows and priority inventory by market.
  • Define channel jobs, voice guardrails, content pillars, approvals, UTMs and reporting baselines.
  • Establish the shared content calendar, approval workflow and weekly performance review.
  • Ship quick wins: Break Alerts, parent-question videos and stronger proof content.

Beyond the first 90 days

Build a local creator network that grows with every city.

Recess does not need one national creator trying to feel local everywhere. It needs a dependable bench of parents, UGC creators and provider partners who can show what programs actually feel like in each market.

01 · CITY BENCH

Trusted local voices

Recruit a small mix of parent creators, dad creators, neighborhood voices and polished UGC talent in Austin, Dallas and Houston.

02 · CONTENT ACCESS

Capture the experience

Use clear briefs for camp tours, first-day reactions, planning journeys, provider profiles and school-break coverage.

03 · ATTRIBUTION

Custom registration paths

Give creators tracked links or referral codes so Recess can compare clicks, searches and registrations—not just views.

04 · MARKET LAUNCH

A repeatable city playbook

Pair local voices with city guides, provider content and school-calendar campaigns whenever Recess enters or deepens a market.
