Scaling into new cities

Do not reinvent the social operation every time Recess adds a market.

Build one central operating system, then give local teams the tools and guardrails to make it relevant in their city.

Central strategy

One Recess brand and operating system.

The social lead owns the shared strategy, standards and learning loop.

  • Brand voice, pillars and visual guardrails
  • Seasonal campaign calendar and master templates
  • Measurement definitions, UTMs and reporting
  • Approval rules, consent and escalation paths
  • Cross-market testing and reusable learnings
Local execution

City coordinators make the system feel native.

Local owners translate the strategy through real providers, calendars, creators and community conversations.

  • Capture local program and parent content
  • Maintain district dates, neighborhoods and timely needs
  • Build provider, school and creator relationships
  • Manage community questions and local sentiment
  • Surface inventory gaps and market-specific opportunities

Social brand guidelines

Give every coordinator a clear wheelhouse—not a script.

The guidelines should protect Recess’s trust and recognizability while giving local creators enough freedom to sound human.

Parent truth first

Begin with a real planning need, emotion or question. Recess is the relief—not the hero of every opening sentence.

Useful beats clever

Humor earns attention, but dates, prices, neighborhoods, schedules and clear next steps earn saves and registrations.

Specific beats generic

Name the city, area, age, interest, timing and use case whenever inventory supports it.

Simple product language

Avoid startup vocabulary. Explain Recess through the job it helps the parent finish.

Warm—never guilt-based

Voice: practical, encouraging, lightly funny and nonjudgmental. Never shame parents for planning late or needing coverage.

Protect family trust

Clear consent, child dignity, accurate provider claims, appropriate disclosures and escalation for sensitive community issues.

Scaling the team

Make the system teachable before adding headcount.

New coordinators should inherit a clear operating model, not a folder of old posts and the cheerful instruction to “make it local.”

Social lead

Sets the system

  • Own strategy, pillars and master calendar
  • Set campaign briefs and success criteria
  • Run reporting and cross-market learning
  • Approve sensitive or major brand work
  • Coach coordinators and resource markets
City coordinator

Owns local relevance

  • Capture local providers and community stories
  • Adapt approved formats to local needs
  • Manage comments, DMs and timely updates
  • Maintain creators, schools and partners
  • Report parent questions and inventory gaps
Broader team

Feeds the engine

  • Growth shares conversion and campaign priorities
  • Support surfaces repeated parent questions
  • Provider teams identify inventory and stories
  • Product clarifies features and friction
  • Founders add trust and point of view

How the system earns investment

Measure both local growth and operational health.

A city is not succeeding because its follower count moved. The useful question is whether content is building trust, qualified discovery and a repeatable local operation.

Attention qualityWatch time, saves, shares and meaningful comments
Local intentCity clicks, search starts and provider-page views
Business outcomeRegistrations, repeat use and assisted bookings
Content supplyProvider, creator and community assets captured
Operating healthCadence reliability, approval time and response time
Scale decision: increase local ownership when provider density, content volume, community demand and registration opportunity justify a coordinator who can spend every day closer to that market.
